What makes land data-center-ready

Not all acreage near a substation qualifies. These criteria are the difference between a site that closes in 90 days and one that stalls for 18 months.

Power

Look for

Substation with available capacity, an interconnection agreement, and redundant A+B feeds.

Red flag

Substation already at capacity, or an interconnection queue running 24+ months.

Zoning and entitlements

Look for

Pre-entitled land — industrial, tech or data-center use.

Red flag

Agricultural or residential zoning requiring full rezoning — 6–24 months plus community approval risk.

Fiber and connectivity

Look for

On or adjacent to an existing long-haul route or dark fiber.

Red flag

Nearest route requires a new build of 5+ miles.

Water and cooling

Look for

Municipal water access with adequate pressure and secured rights.

Red flag

Arid region, no water rights secured, and no alternative cooling plan.

Climate and disaster risk

Look for

Seismic zone 0–1 and FEMA Zone X. Cooler ambient temperatures also cut PUE.

Red flag

Seismic zone 3–4, or FEMA Zone A / AE — the 100-year flood plain.

Topography and access

Look for

Flat land with highway access. 40+ acres for a meaningful facility, 200+ for hyperscale.

Red flag

Under 10 acres — limits build-out and expansion.

The 2026 land market

Powered land, with a utility LOA in place, carries a meaningful premium over unentitled acreage in every market. That premium is the interconnection work already done.

Premium markets

$1M – $4M+ / acre

You pay for adjacency — existing fiber density, established peering, enterprise proximity. Power is the constraint, and it's why the price is what it is.

MarketPrice / acreTrendNote
Northern Virginia$2M – $4M+↑↑Amazon paid ~$3.7M/acre for 189 acres, Nov 2025. Power moratorium caps new supply.
Silicon Valley$1M – $3M+↑Highest US cost outside NoVA. Land scarcity and permitting compress supply.

Growth markets

$100K – $500K / acre

Appreciating fastest, because this is where the primary markets' overflow is going. Still buyable, but the window is the thing that's closing.

MarketPrice / acreTrendNote
Phoenix$200K – $500K↑↑ 40–50%Fastest-appreciating secondary market. Water offset by solar.
Columbus / Ohio$150K – $300K↑↑ 3–5×Farmland at $10–15K/acre now exceeds $150K once rezoned.

Reno$100K – $250K↑ 20–40%California relief valve. Low taxes, cool climate.

Power-rich markets

$5K – $200K / acreMost of our listings are here

Land stops being the constraint. What you're buying is grid capacity and a utility that will commit to a date — and at these prices the interconnection work, not the acreage, is the whole cost.

MarketPrice / acreTrendNote
Atlanta$50K – $200K↑As low as $50K in outer metros; inner suburbs appreciating fast.
Dallas / DFW$50K – $150K↑Most accessible large-market pricing. Rising near substations.
Wyoming / Idaho / rural West$5K – $50K→Lowest US cost for power-rich greenfield. Power and fiber, not land price, is the limit.

The spread between the two ends is roughly 800×. What closes most of that gap isn't the land — it's whether the interconnection work has already been done.
